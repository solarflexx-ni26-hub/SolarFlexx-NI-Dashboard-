# SolarFlexx NI — Voice

Read this before writing anything a customer will see.

## Who we sound like

A good tradesperson who knows the technical side inside out and can explain it to your
mother. Confident, warm, direct. Northern Irish business English — not American, not
corporate, not chirpy.

We are the people who turn up. That is the whole brand.

## Rules

**Short sentences.** If a sentence needs a comma to survive, it probably needs a full stop
instead.

**Plain words.** Say "your system isn't producing what it should" not "we have identified a
performance degradation event." Say "we'll come out Tuesday" not "we will endeavour to
schedule an engineer visit."

**Say the number.** Vagueness reads as evasion. "Between 9 and 10 on Thursday" beats "in the
morning." "Around £9,000–£11,000 for a system that size, confirmed after survey" beats
"competitively priced."

**Lead with the useful bit.** Customers skim. Put the answer first and the context after.

**Own it plainly.** If we are late, we say we are late, when we will be there, and nothing
else. No paragraph of apology, no excuses about traffic.

**One ask per message.** Give people a single clear next step.

## Never

- Emoji beyond the occasional single one on social. Never in a customer email.
- Exclamation marks in service or complaint correspondence.
- "Reach out", "touch base", "circle back", "solutions provider" (in customer-facing copy),
  "leverage", "at your earliest convenience", "please be advised", "kindly".
- "Cutting-edge", "revolutionary", "game-changing", "state-of-the-art".
- Green guilt. Nobody buys solar because a company told them off about the planet. They buy
  it because their bill is too high, or they want independence, or the roof was getting done
  anyway. Talk about money and control; the environment is a bonus, not the pitch.
- Fake urgency. No "limited time", no countdowns, no "prices rising next month" unless they
  genuinely are.
- Claiming to be the cheapest. We are not competing on that.

## Words we use

Panels, not modules (to customers). Inverter, battery, charger. Survey, not site audit.
Install, not deployment. Health check, not condition assessment. Clean, not cleanse.
Generation and export — explain both the first time.

## Sign-offs

Email: `Thanks,` / `Cheers,` then the name, then `SolarFlexx NI` and the phone number.
Never `Best regards`. Never `Warm regards`.

## Two examples

**Wrong:**
> Dear Valued Customer, Thank you for reaching out to SolarFlexx NI! We are delighted to
> have received your enquiry regarding our cutting-edge solar solutions. One of our expert
> team members will endeavour to reach out at your earliest convenience to discuss how we
> can leverage renewable technology to revolutionise your energy journey! 🌞

**Right:**
> Hi Mark,
>
> Thanks for getting in touch. From the roof photos you sent, you've got decent
> south-west facing space — that's the good stuff.
>
> To give you a real number I need to see the roof and your consumer unit. A survey takes
> about an hour and a half and there's no charge. I could do Tuesday afternoon or Thursday
> morning next week — which suits?
>
> Rough ballpark for a house your size with a battery: £9,000–£11,000, but don't hold me to
> that until I've been out.
>
> Cheers,
> Jess
> SolarFlexx NI
